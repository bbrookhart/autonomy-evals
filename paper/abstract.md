# Preserving Human Agency: Evaluating Epistemic and Decision Autonomy in Multi-Turn AI Assistants

We introduce a behavioral evaluation framework studying evidence grounding, counterevidence recovery, preference-sensitive deliberation and action pressure in fictional multi-turn assistant interactions. Controlled position pairs hold evidence fixed; crossed confidence and validation conditions test response sensitivity. Four system instruction arms permit evaluation of autonomy-related behavior alongside independent helpfulness dimensions. The framework includes blinded multi-judge and human annotation workflows, scenario-clustered uncertainty and transparent failure reporting. These behavioral proxies do not establish actual human harm. Results pending execution and measurement validation.
